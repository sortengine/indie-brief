Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Thursday 29 October.

Realscreen reports that Firelight Media has named its Documentary Lab cohort for 2026. The New York-based production company has selected 10 fellows for the program, which provides participants with 25,000 dollars in funding.

Also from Realscreen, Channel 4 has appointed Alisa Pomeroy as its new head of unscripted, taking up the role in January. Pomeroy will report to the recently appointed director of programmes as the UK pubcaster retools its unscripted department.

Realscreen also reports that STV Studios Factual has rebranded as North Star. The Glasgow-based production company made the move following the appointment of its new creative director Mark Hedgecoe earlier this month.

In documentary news, Realscreen reports that Lance Oppenheim and Nathan Fielder's A24 documentary You Can See Everything is among the shortlist for International Documentary Association nominations, alongside Laura Poitras and Rachel Lauren Mueller's They're Here. The list includes 16 features and 12 shorts.

Screen Daily reports that director Peter Medak, known for The Changeling and The Krays, has died at 88. Medak was a prolific filmmaker who worked across drama and documentary throughout his career.

And in a significant development for Iranian cinema, Screen Daily reports that filmmaker Jafar Panahi, director of the Palme d'Or-winning and Oscar-nominated It Was Just an Accident, faces a five-year prison sentence in Iran. Panahi is currently in the country.
