Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Saturday, 7 November.

Screen Daily reports that the American Film Market is underway at a critical time for the independent sector. Attendees are mulling several major developments: the effects of the Paramount and Warner Bros Discovery merger, a proposed US federal tax incentive, and the launch of TIFF: The Market. Buyers are described as cautious as they navigate this uncertain landscape.

In documentary news, Realscreen reports that HBO has slated a five-episode docu-comedy series called Oobah: Scams For a Better Tomorrow, premiering November 22. The series comes from A24, Abso Lutely Productions and Central, and follows filmmaker and UK journalist Oobah Butler.

Also on the doc front, Modern Times Review highlights James Longley's Mohabat, winner of the main prize at Ji.hlava 2026. The film follows three Afghan brothers after an American drone strike kills their father, with one sent to a Taliban-run orphanage and another to a drug treatment centre.

Screen Daily reports that Canadian filmmaker Bruno Dumont is turning to generative AI to film an English-language science fiction thriller called The End. Dumont said: "I'm like a director working with a crew, except that my crew is a machine."

On the financing front, Realscreen reports that the Canadian federal government has committed 300 million Canadian dollars to CBC Radio-Canada over the next two fiscal years as part of a 4 billion dollar spending plan on arts and culture.

Finally, Screen Daily reports on Hungary's film industry prospects under a new government. Fresh appointees have been ushered in and a crucial financial incentive for producers has been stabilised, raising questions about whether the country can rebuild trust in the post-Orban era.
