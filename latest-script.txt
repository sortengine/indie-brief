Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Tuesday 6 October.

Realscreen reports that Channel 4 has appointed Louisa Compton, director of news, digital and audience trust, to the channel's board as an executive member in a newly created director of programmes role. The appointment reflects the pubcaster's evolving leadership structure as it navigates the current media landscape.

In major industry restructuring news, the Supreme Court has denied a last-minute effort to halt the Paramount and Warner Bros Discovery merger, which is scheduled to close on Tuesday. Separately, Skydance has revealed its executive team for the combined company. Screen Daily reports that Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein have been confirmed to run merged film operations, while Casey Bloys heads streaming.

Moving to commissions, Realscreen reports that Channel 5 has greenlit a new docuseries from London-based Blackmane Media featuring explorer Levison Wood. The series chronicles a four-month, ten thousand mile journey across South America.

On the festival circuit, the Busan International Film Festival has received a significant boost. Deadline reports that the festival has been designated as a pathway to Oscar nominations after receiving recognition from the International Federation of Film Producers Associations, joining their list of A-list festivals. Festival director Jung Hanseok has spoken about the achievement and the festival's role in supporting Korean cinema recovery.

Finally, Realscreen reports that Banijay Entertainment has solidified its newly combined structure across the Americas region following the recent merger with All3Media. Ben Samek remains as CEO of what is now Banijay America, and the company has launched a production services hub and new Latin America division.
