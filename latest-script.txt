Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Tuesday 24 November.

Realscreen reports that Silverback Films, the Bristol-based Banijay UK-owned production company behind Ocean with David Attenborough, has promoted three senior figures, including elevating Jane Hamlin to CEO. Hamlin was previously chief operating officer and director of production.

Also in leadership moves, Realscreen reports that Passion, the London-based group of unscripted production companies, has named David Moulton as its new CEO, replacing Nick Southgate who led the company for five years.

The British Independent Film Awards has unveiled its craft category winners ahead of next month's ceremony. Deadline reports that Clio Barnard's I See Buildings Fall Like Lightning, which follows five lifelong friends at age 30, took three awards including Best Casting for Shaheen Baig.

In documentary news, Deadline reports that Netflix Brazil has set a release date for March of the Jaguars, the streamer's first wildlife documentary from Latin America. The feature was created and produced in Brazil by Alexandre Soullier and Emmanuel Priou, who won the Oscar for Best Documentary Feature for March of the Penguins.

Realscreen reports that Dogwoof, the London-based independent documentary producer and distributor, is partnering with Tattle TV to reimagine two of its true-crime documentaries for vertical, mobile-first audiences using AI-assisted adaptation.

And Modern Times Review reports that Verzi-ó 2026 has named My Word Against Mine, a film by Maasja Ooms about hearing voices, as Best International Documentary. The winning films screen until 25 November before online Verzi-ó begins on 26 November.
