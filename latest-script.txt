Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Sunday 1 November.

Modern Times Review reports that James Longley's film Mohabat has taken the main award at the 30th Ji.hlava International Documentary Film Festival. The festival also announced that Trinh T. Minh-ha has received the Contribution to World Cinema Award, recognizing her significant impact on documentary and experimental cinema.

In industry news from the same festival, Modern Times Review also covers the Ji.hlava Industry Awards for 2026, which went to thirteen documentary projects selected from the New Visions Forum. The awards programme, led by Belgium's 6TM and the American project Searching for SmarterChild, highlights emerging work across the documentary sector.

The Portuguese festival Porto Post Doc has unveiled its 2026 competition programme according to Modern Times Review. The festival has selected eight films for its International Competition and is programming a dedicated music strand featuring everything from Brazilian samba to West Berlin's underground scene, demonstrating how documentary festivals are expanding thematic programming beyond traditional approaches.

Also from Modern Times Review, Andrea Culková's documentary Dear People, Dear Citizens offers a creative take on the Conference on the Future of Europe, transforming the EU citizens' assembly into an exploration of democratic renewal and participation.

Finally, POV Magazine reviews Captain Newfoundland, a new documentary by Mike Feehan and Jamie Miller charting the eccentric history of Geoff Stiring, who created both Newfoundland Television and its legendary character Captain Newfoundland, a fixture in Canadian popular culture since nineteen fifty-five.
