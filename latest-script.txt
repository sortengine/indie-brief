Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Thursday 12 November.

Barbara Broccoli, the legendary James Bond producer, has been appointed as the first female president of the UK's National Film and Television School. The appointment comes just weeks before the opening of a building dedicated to her father, the iconic Cubby Broccoli. Broccoli brings a longstanding relationship with the NFTS to the role.

Screen Daily reports that inward film spend in the UK has dropped 40 percent from January to September 2026, compared to the same period last year. However, there's a silver lining: co-production activity is up 31 percent. Spend on local UK features was down 17 percent year-on-year.

Realscreen reports that PBS is commissioning the four-part Secrets of the Ancient Wonders series from UK-based Blink Films, set for 2027. The series uses LiDAR scanning and CGI technology to visualise ancient historical sites.

The Grierson British Documentary Awards announced its winners this week. Screen Daily reports that the BBC and Netflix were the big winners at the 54th ceremony, with the BBC taking home four awards. Documentary highlights included Grenfell Uncovered and Tale of Silyan.

In a positive sign for the documentary sector, Screen Daily reports on comments from Isabel Arrate Fernandez, head of IDFA, who points to the success of recent documentaries like NAZA as proof that audiences should not be underestimated.

Finally, the American Film Market is running through November 15th, with buyers and sellers converging on the marketplace.
