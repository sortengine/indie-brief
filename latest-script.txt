Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Friday 20 November.

Starting with a significant development for European filmmakers. Screen Daily reports that Members of the European Parliament have proposed a funding increase for the EU's ringfenced Media programme, with moves expected to lead to dedicated financing of at least two billion euros for film and TV-focused initiatives. That's substantial backing for the independent sector across the continent.

Turning to documentaries and festivals. Deadline reports that the David Lean documentary Maverick: The Epic Adventures of David Lean, narrated by Cate Blanchett, has sold to multiple key territories following its world premiere in Cannes Classics. MetFilm Distribution is releasing the film in the UK and Ireland from December 4th, with deals secured in France, Germany, Spain and Australia. Meanwhile, the Doha Film Festival has opened despite regional geopolitical tensions, with organisers stating they chose not to cancel despite a difficult year.

On the commissioning front, Deadline reports that Epis, the vertical video streamer, is developing two true crime originals from Silvio Productions: The Swindler and the Billionaire Murder and Two Marys. This marks the first time a microdrama-style service has created premium true crime content.

BBC Studios Unscripted is launching a new digital production label, according to Realscreen. The unnamed label will focus on creating digitally native intellectual property and securing commissions from platforms and broadcasters.

Finally, the Criterion Channel has acquired three short films by London-based Nigerian British filmmaker Tomisin Adepeju. Appreciation, Journey Mercies, and Lift Me Up will be available to stream from December 1st.

That's your briefing for today.
