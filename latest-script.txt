Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Monday two November.

Deadline reports that Emmy-winning producer-directors Gesbeen Mohammad and Robin Barnwell have launched a new independent production company called Sisu Pictures. The indie is dedicated to telling major stories of the year, starting with two documentaries airing back-to-back on the BBC and Channel Four. Iran: The People's Story and another doc will premiere on the same evening, marking an ambitious launch for the company which has previously won Peabodys and BAFTAs alongside their Emmy awards.

Also from Deadline: the documentary series The Real Wolf of Wall Street is expanding internationally. Hat Trick International has secured deals with the BBC in the UK and Stan in Australia for the three-part Paramount Plus series about broker Jordan Belfort. The show was launched at MIPCOM Cannes earlier this month, with Maxine Productions among the co-producers.

Modern Times Review features an interview with film curator Dina Pokrajac about the Subversive festival in Zagreb. The festival, which has a long and radical history, was conceived from the start to combine cinema with political theory and practice, drawing inspiration from movements like Third Cinema.

And POV Magazine reports that Matthew Rankin's film Ladies and Gentlemen, Brian Mulroney has won the WIFF Prize in Canadian Film at Windsor International Film Festival, along with a twenty-five thousand dollar cash prize.
