Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Sunday 11 October.

Deadline reports that the Producers Guild of Korea and the Sakha Filmmakers Association have signed a memorandum of understanding to create a direct route for international co-productions and professional exchange. The Sakha Republic in Russia's Far East has developed one of the country's most dynamic screen production sectors, and this partnership opens new opportunities for Korean and Sakha producers to collaborate on joint projects.

In festival news, Screen Daily reports that the UK has selected its best international feature Oscar entry. The film won the L'Oeil d'or at Cannes for best documentary across all sections, marking significant recognition for British filmmaking on the international stage.

Also from Screen Daily, Northern Irish composer Hannah Peel has picked up the discovery of the year award at the 2026 World Soundtrack Awards. Peel was recognised for her work on Project Hail Mary, highlighting the growing importance of original scoring in independent and international productions.

On the actor-turned-director front, Deadline reports that Rohan Campbell is planning to make his directorial debut next summer with what he describes as a very personal story. The Below star is currently awaiting greenlight for the project, signalling another emerging talent moving behind the camera.

Finally, Nicolas Cage told New York Comic-Con that he did not sign an AI waiver for Spider-Noir and said he probably won't work again with Prime Video because Amazon is very AI-friendly. His stance reflects growing concerns in the independent production community about artificial intelligence rights and actor protection.
