Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Sunday 8 November.

Deadline reports that Taiwan Creative Content Agency has been showcasing the projects backed by four jointly established content funds. The Chunghwa Digital Cultural and Creative Fund, Momomini Kids Content Fund, Joint Journey Creative and Enjoy Entertainment presented their investments at Taiwan Creative Content Fest. The slate includes projects such as Our Blues, Midnight Diner and C'est La Vie. This represents a significant push by Taiwanese financiers into content production.

Also from Taiwan, Mirror Fiction has announced fifty thousand dollars in awards for book-to-screen adaptations. The company distributed the prizes to two authors at Taiwan Creative Content Fest to support both publication and screen adaptation of their work. This is the fifth edition of Mirror Fiction's biennial awards programme, highlighting growing opportunities for literary IP development in the region.

Chunghwa Telecom, one of three major Taiwanese telcos investing in film and television, has unveiled its own investment slate at the festival. Projects include the police crime drama Gunshot starring Kent Tsai and Call Of Lobster featuring Choi Siwon and Chang Chen. These investments demonstrate the continued appetite from Asian telecoms for premium content.

Deadline reports that Christopher Nolan, now serving as DGA president, is pushing studios to protect copyrighted materials from artificial intelligence. The director has included anti-AI boilerplate language in his current project The Odyssey and is urging studios to actively pursue legal action against AI recreations of their work.

Finally, Modern Times Review reports that Marnie Blok's debut short Beyond Silence has won the MIOB In Shorts Award for twenty twenty-six. The film, about a deaf PhD student reporting sexual abuse by a professor, took the one thousand euro audience prize.
