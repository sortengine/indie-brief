Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Tuesday 20 October.

Screen Daily reports that Mia Bays, director of the BFI Filmmaking Fund, is coming to the end of her five-year tenure. Bays has corrected the record about her role, noting that people often thought she ran the London Film Festival, the archive, and BFI Southbank as well. She's feeling energised as she prepares to move on from the position.

In documentary news, Realscreen reports that A24's You Can See Everything, the three-hour documentary by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes, enjoyed the biggest box office opening for a documentary in 13 years at the North American box office. The film's strong performance is generating awards season discussion.

Boat Rocker Studios has closed new United States deals for its critically acclaimed sci-fi drama series Orphan Black. Under co-exclusive pacts, all five seasons are now available on HBO Max and Peacock. The Emmy-winning series stars Tatiana Maslany.

Realscreen also reports that the BBC Studios Natural History Unit is behind a new weekly television series celebrating British wildlife called Naturewatch, which premieres on November 6 on BBC Two and BBC iPlayer, with a companion podcast also in the works.

One Tribe TV, based in Wales and Bath, is launching a new YouTube channel aimed at showcasing films from emerging natural history and science filmmakers from the UK and around the world.

And finally, Belgian formats distributor Primitives has revived the entertainment format Boxing Stars in the Netherlands after an eight-year gap. The new edition is produced by Signal.Stream and will launch shortly.
