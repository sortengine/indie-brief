Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Friday 30 October.

Realscreen reports that Earthrise and Bupa have launched the Planet Health Film Fund, a one hundred thousand pound initiative supporting independent documentary filmmakers. Five filmmakers will each receive twenty thousand pounds to make short documentaries on health-related topics. This is a significant funding opportunity for producers in the documentary space.

Also in commissioning news, Realscreen reports that Channel 4 has appointed Adam Adler as creative director for its in-house production team. Adler, a former entertainment commissioner at the broadcaster, will help build out Channel 4's internal production capabilities, signalling the pubcaster's commitment to increasing original content production.

In sales and distribution, Deadline reports that Korean sales company Finecut has closed multiple territorial deals for two films. Journey There, starring Justin H. Min, has sold to territories including Japan, Taiwan, Mongolia and Israel. The company has also continued selling Hong Sangsoo's Locarno Film Festival awards winner Nowhere to Lay My Eyes to additional markets. These deals highlight ongoing international appetite for independent and festival-selected films.

On the labour front, Deadline reports that SAG-AFTRA members have ratified new television animation contracts, securing a twelve point six percent wage increase over four years. The agreements also include enhanced protections around artificial intelligence, an increasingly important consideration for producers working in animation.

Finally, Deadline reports that the Writers Guild, SAG-AFTRA and the American Federation of Musicians have jointly filed an amicus brief asking a federal judge to reject fair use exemptions for training AI models without licensing. The unions are warning against the harms of unlicensed AI training, an issue with significant implications for independent creators and producers.
