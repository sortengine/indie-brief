Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Saturday 31 October.

Realscreen reports that Channel 4 and Disney Plus have struck a major content partnership designed to extend digital distribution for both parties. The collaboration represents the latest in a series of broadcaster and streamer deals reshaping how independent producers can access multiple platforms for their work.

In documentary news, the International Documentary Association has issued a statement regarding the sentencing of Iranian filmmaker Jafar Panahi. The IDA continues to advocate for filmmakers facing persecution globally.

Also in documentary, Realscreen reports that Investigation Discovery has slated a four-part docuseries on Andrew Tate for a November 25 premiere across the network and HBO Max. Meanwhile, HBO has scheduled a November 17 premiere for the documentary When a Witness Recants, directed by Dawn Porter with Ta-Nehisi Coates as executive producer. The film previously premiered at Sundance.

On the festival circuit, Modern Times Review reports that the eleventh MOLDOX International Documentary Film Festival returns to Chişinău from November 25 to 29. This year's edition includes the festival's first retrospective, devoted to the work of Agnès Varda, and explores the question of coexistence.

In production news, Realscreen reports that Skydance has solidified its senior executive team structure across studios, sports and streaming divisions, with JB Perrette announcing key appointments across international and domestic operations. The company is also searching for a new head of its Pluto and AVOD division.

Finally, Fred Rogers Productions, the non-profit founded by the children's entertainer, is partnering with media studio The Mash-Up Americans to produce Young Neighbors, its first podcast project.

That's Indie Brief. Have a productive weekend.
