Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Sunday 25 October.

We start with significant news for independent filmmakers at major festivals. Screen Daily reports that Clio Barnard's Birmingham-set drama I See Buildings Fall Like Lightning has taken the top prize at Rome Film Festival in the Progressive Cinema competition. A major achievement for British independent cinema on the European festival circuit.

In documentary news, Modern Times Review highlights Julia Loktev's ambitious project My Undesirable Friends. Shot across two parts totalling nearly six hours, the film documents the journalists of TV Rain as Russia's independent media faces shutdown and reporters scatter into exile. An important work capturing the reality of independent media under pressure.

Moving to production development, Deadline reports that creators Erin and Sara Foster have lined up a new series project at Hulu called These Summer Storms. The drama, based on Sarah MacLean's forthcoming 2025 novel, is in development at Hulu through 20th Television under the Fosters' Fatigue Sisters production banner. This comes as their Netflix series Nobody Wants This launches its third season this weekend.

On the labour front, SAG-AFTRA has voted to extend National Executive Director Duncan Crabtree-Ireland's contract through June 2032, with his title updated to include CEO. The vote passed with ninety-eight point three-four percent approval during Saturday's National Board meeting.

Finally, a reminder that if you're producing drama or comedy for international streamers or broadcasters, the commissioning landscape continues to favour established producers with development pipelines. The success of independent creators like the Fosters in securing multiple projects demonstrates the value of maintaining strong relationships with major distributors and platforms.

That's Indie Brief for today. Stay independent.
