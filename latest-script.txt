Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Thursday 1 October.

A significant structural shift in the industry: Screen Daily reports that the Paramount-Warner Bros Discovery merger has been approved, with the deal expected to close on October 6th. David Ellison has named Ynon Kreiz as co-CEO of the combined company. This consolidation will reshape the landscape for independent producers seeking distribution and financing partnerships.

On the funding front, Realscreen reports that the Better Angels Society has announced the five winners of the 2026 Better Angels Lavine Fellowship, which supports documentary projects about American history. If you're working on a historical documentary, this is worth investigating.

A cautionary tale for the UK independent sector: Screen Daily confirms that True Brit, the high-profile UK distributor and producer-financier, has gone into administration. Founder Zygi Kamasa has confirmed the news. This is a significant loss for the independent film community and a reminder of the distribution challenges facing arthouse content.

Looking at production trends, Realscreen reports that Boat Rocker Studios has added three unscripted originals to its production slate ahead of MIPCOM, with the company fully financing all three productions. This suggests continued appetite for factual programming from major producers.

On the documentary festival circuit, Screen Daily notes that Festival do Rio, running from October 1st to 11th, will open with the Cannes award-winning Spanish Oscar submission La Bola Negra. The festival's head Ilda Santiago is highlighting how Brazilian cinema's elite profile is fuelling growth in the market.

And finally, Screen Daily asks an important question for the independent community: can films make good money from a decent festival run? As distribution becomes increasingly hard to come by, festivals are becoming a crucial way for arthouse films to connect with audiences and generate revenue.
