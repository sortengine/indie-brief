Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Thursday 19 November.

Realscreen reports that the International Documentary Festival Amsterdam's Forum Awards were revealed this week, with Pallavi Paul's How Love Moves receiving the Al Jazeera Documentary Co-Production Award. Meanwhile, Asmahan Bkerat's Jordan-Palestine co-production Concrete Land is leading the nominations for this year's IDA Documentary Awards, with the ceremony taking place December 4th in Los Angeles.

In co-production news, Deadline reports that Qatar's Film Committee at Media City Qatar has signed a memorandum of understanding with UAE-based streaming service Yango Play to explore development, co-production and distribution of scripted and unscripted Arabic-language originals. The agreement kicks off with a falconry documentary series called Elzam and was announced at Doha Industry Days 2026.

Also from Doha, beIN Media Group and NBCUniversal Global have reinforced their relationship with a new multi-year output agreement covering recent releases and awards winners heading to MENA audiences.

On the UK funding front, Screen Daily is reporting on how new regional funds might help level up film production across England, with industry figures optimistic about opportunities for their regions.

Turning to festivals and submissions, Modern Times Review reports that DOK.fest München is now open for entries for its 42nd edition running in May 2027, with documentaries welcome from filmmakers, producers, students and composers.

And Realscreen reports that Fremantle is extending its partnership with Zinc Media Group with an agreement to expand both companies' digital ecosystems.
