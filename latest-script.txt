Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 25 November.

Deadline reports that the UK actors union Equity is preparing to enter negotiations with Netflix, Disney and Apple early next year. This comes as Equity nears completion of its long-awaited deal with producer trade body Pact. The streamer talks are expected to begin at the start of 2025.

The British film and television industry is bracing for the impact of a new US federal film and TV tax incentive. Deadline has learned that UK trade bodies, industry organizations and studios are watching nervously as the proposal advances with strong political backing. Industry figures fear the incentive could divert production away from the UK.

Meanwhile, Realscreen reports that the Academy has revealed 190 films eligible for its 2027 documentary feature award, including A24's You Can See Everything. This represents a significant slate for documentary makers and distributors to consider as awards season takes shape.

Realscreen also brings news that international producer Bomanbridge Media has closed more than 500 hours of content sales across Asia-Pacific. The deals cover factual, natural history, lifestyle, premium arts and children's programming, demonstrating strong appetite for quality non-fiction content in the region.

In festival news, the International Emmys have wrapped with honours for UK productions including Louis Theroux: The Settlers and Educating Yorkshire, underlining the strength of British documentary and observational programming on the global stage.

Finally, BBC One has commissioned Celebrity Parlour Games, a Christmas special from Tuesday's Child based on an original idea from Curious Cat. The special will air on BBC One and BBC iPlayer.
