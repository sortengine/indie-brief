Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 18 November.

Qatar's Film Committee is moving from vision to execution. The body overseeing the country's film and TV business is gearing up for the second edition of its Industry Days, running from November 19 to 20 alongside the Doha Film Festival. The inaugural edition last year unveiled long-gestated plans, and this year promises to build on that momentum with a 50 percent film and TV cash incentive.

Screen Daily reports that Skydance is developing an indie film fund as part of a new studio leadership structure. The company says the theatrical business is central to its vision for the future.

Realscreen reports that the BBC is unveiling a new Entertainment group structure, designed to simplify commissioning under the entertainment umbrella while also supercharging development and strengthening duty of care processes.

The Dardenne brothers are receiving the European Film Academy's Lifetime Achievement Award at the 39th European Film Awards in January 2027. The multi-award-winning Belgian directorial duo are among the most internationally acclaimed filmmakers to emerge from Europe.

And Realscreen also reports that the Independent Television Service has announced a production call for feature-length documentaries with its PBS strand Independent Lens. Selected filmmakers will partner with ITVS and PBS for support.
