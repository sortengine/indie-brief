Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Monday 9 November.

Deadline reports that You Can See Everything has swept the top categories at the Critics Choice Documentary Awards. Nathan Fielding and Lance Oppenheim's documentary about Elizabeth Holmes in her final days before prison took every award in which it was nominated, including the top prize.

Also on the festival circuit, Modern Times Review covers the Bozcaada International Festival of Ecological Documentary, highlighting the conversations that extend beyond the screenings themselves. And Doclisboa's Recovering Images of Palestine project brings together confiscated and miraculously exhumed films that speak to Gaza today through archival Palestinian footage.

At the Taiwan Creative Content Fest, Deadline reports that Tan Ce Ding's Somewhere In The South won the top pitching award, the TAICCA x CNC Feature Film Grand Award, worth thirty-five thousand dollars. The film is a co-production between Malaysia-based Evil Genius Studio and Singapore-based Giraffe Pictures.

Moving to international co-productions, Fifth Season has secured European deals for ITV's drama The Blame, starring Michelle Keegan and Douglas Booth. The series has sold to broadcasters across nine territories including Sweden, Norway, Slovenia, Belgium, Ireland, Portugal and the Netherlands.

And at the Marrakech International Film Festival, Jeff Goldblum, John Turturro and Moroccan composer-actor Younès Mégri have been announced as honorees for this year's twenty-third edition. Filmmaker Rian Johnson and James Gray will participate in festival conversations.
