Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 14 October.

Starting with documentary festivals and markets. Realscreen reports that the International Documentary Film Festival Amsterdam has revealed its full competition lineups for the 2026 edition, with a film about Myanmar's resistance movement as the opening title. Meanwhile, the Krakow Film Festival is opening submissions for its 67th edition, adding a new International Documentary Debut Competition to its programme running from 30 May to 6 June 2027. And the FIFDH Impact Days programme has named twelve documentaries for its 2027 Impact Lab, gathering projects from around the world covering subjects from human trafficking in Europe to rising seas in Kerala.

On the commissioning front, Realscreen reports the BBC has lined up a two-part docuseries on Harry Potter author J.K. Rowling from production company Rogan Scotland, set to air on BBC Two. And National Geographic has appointed former Plimsoll Studios executive Alex Ranken as a commissioning editor based in the UK.

For documentary acquisitions and distribution, Deadline reports the New York Times Op-Docs platform has acquired The Revolution Against Death, a new short film by two-time Oscar nominee Joshua Oppenheimer, set at an annual longevity conference. The film will be co-released globally in early December with MUBI.

On industry support, the International Documentary Association has announced its Emergency Assistance programme is now permanent, providing ongoing support for documentary filmmakers.

And in policy news, Screen Daily reports Australian producers are stepping up calls for the removal of a twenty percent cap on the key Producer Offset tax rebate, arguing that above-the-line costs represent more than twenty percent of total budgets.
