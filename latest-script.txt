Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Saturday 3 October.

Realscreen reports that DOC NYC, America's largest documentary film festival, has unveiled the programmes for DOC NYC PRO, its seven-day industry conference focused on the art and business of documentary. The conference will also feature an inaugural Solutions Lab, offering producers, financiers and distributors a dedicated space for deal-making and development.

In festival news, Modern Times Review reports that the 23rd Verzió Film Festival opens in Budapest on November 16 with more than eighty films. The festival, shaped by the question of whether seeing is still believing, has received its first-ever grant from the National Film Institute.

Deadline reports that NAZA, the Venice Prize-winning documentary written and directed by Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, has started its US theatrical run. The Gaza exposé received a special jury prize at its Venice premiere last month with a record 25-minute standing ovation and is now playing at the IFC Center in New York.

Speaking of major director debuts, Deadline also reports on Pedro Pascal's praise for Tony Gilroy's feature film Behemoth, which premiered at the New York Film Festival. Pascal said Gilroy made the intimate drama feel surprisingly healing after 14 years away from feature directing.

There's significant corporate restructuring across the production sector. Realscreen reports that Sharon Levy is exiting Endemol Shine North America after close to a decade with the Banijay-owned production company, most recently as CEO. Meanwhile, Sony Pictures Television has announced a new distribution team following the retirement of Mike Wald, co-president of distribution and networks, at year end.

And Realscreen confirms that the Paramount-Warner Bros Discovery combined company will be named Skydance. The merger is set to close on Tuesday.
