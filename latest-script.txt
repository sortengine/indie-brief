Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Friday 16 October.

Screen Daily reports that Tessa Ross has said even successful producers are struggling to raise funding. Speaking to an audience in Rome, she noted that having made a film that worked doesn't guarantee anyone will trust you to make another one. It's a sobering reminder of the current state of independent film financing.

On the commissioning front, Realscreen reports that Hearst Networks UK and Canada's Super Channel have greenlit the first two seasons of the true crime series Missing Pieces from Manitoba-based Black Watch Entertainment. Each season will consist of ten episodes.

In union news, Realscreen reports that Noel McClean has been appointed the next head of Bectu, the UK's media and entertainment union, succeeding Philippa Childs who is retiring. McClean will formally take over in January 2027.

Screen Daily reports that Majid Majidi's film Midday Meal has won the top award at Busan, as the festival reached its biggest audience since 2015. Other winners included Taiwanese director Lee Yun Chan for To Be Ordinary.

And Screen Daily has an exclusive: Edem Kelman's film Chairman, starring Shopé Dírísu, has wrapped its London and Paris shoot for Film4 and the BFI, with Samuel Adewunmi and Heather Agyepong also among the cast.

Finally, Modern Times Review reports that the Women and the World International Film Festival runs from five to twelve November, opening with the film Lilly and giving Sigrid Andrea P. Bernardo's Laya its world premiere ahead of the film's national premiere in the Philippines.

That's Indie Brief, keeping you across the industry. Back tomorrow.
