Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Monday 26 October.

Modern Times Review reports that the CME Social Impact Center has backed Michał Marczak's documentary Closure ahead of its 9 November release. The Sundance-premiered film will be paired with a regional campaign focused on loneliness among young people, marking the first acquisition by CME's new impact arm.

In market news, Rome's audiovisual market MIA closed its twelfth edition on 23 October with submissions up 26 percent year on year. The documentary development award went to Loving Ika, a Swedish project exploring artificial intelligence and intimacy. The event attracted over 28 hundred professionals.

Turning to the specialty box office, Deadline reports that Neon-acquired documentary NAZA has passed the one million dollar mark in its theatrical run. The Venice-premiering film from directors Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, two of the Oscar-winning directors of No Other Land, took 74 and a half thousand dollars on 73 screens in its fourth week. Meanwhile, the documentary La Bola Negra continues its limited expansion with strong performances.

And finally, Screen Daily reports that indie distributor A24's You Can See Everything climbed in its second weekend at the US box office, demonstrating sustained audience interest in independent releases.
