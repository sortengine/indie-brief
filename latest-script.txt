Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Thursday 8 October.

Screen Daily reports that the BFI is celebrating outgoing fund head Mia Bays and introducing Isabel Davis at a pre-London Film Festival handover event. BFI CEO Ben Roberts urged supporters to back the National Lottery, saying the organisation needs people to buy tickets.

In funding news, Screen Daily reports that France is doubling Oscar campaign support as the CNC launches an export strategy overhaul. The CNC is now doubling the funding available for Oscar campaigns for French films across all categories, a significant boost for producers looking to pursue international recognition.

Realscreen reports that the Independent Television Service, or ITVS, has partnered with Kanopy, the streaming service for universities and public libraries, via a unique presale model. The initiative is designed to support ITVS co-productions and offers a new distribution pathway for documentary producers.

Also from Realscreen, ITV Studios has launched a new development pod with production company Great Shout and signed a first-look deal with Afro-Mic Productions. The partnerships represent new opportunities for emerging and established independent producers within the ITV Studios ecosystem.

In festival news, Realscreen reports that with MIPCOM just a week away, distributors are presenting their annual Shoppers Guide highlighting scores of new formats and specialist factual content available for commissioning.

And finally, Deadline reports that A24's documentary You Can See Everything, directed by Lance Oppenheim and Nathan Fielding about Elizabeth Holmes and Theranos, is setting records at Alamo Drafthouse with presales that are the highest of any A24 title and any documentary to play the venue.
