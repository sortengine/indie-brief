Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Tuesday 3 November.

Deadline reports that the British Independent Film Awards have unveiled their nominations, with Simon Stone's Elsinore leading the charge with twelve nominations. Andrew Scott is in the running for Best Lead Performance, while Stone himself is nominated for Best Director. The film also picked up nominations for Best British Independent Film and Best Screenplay.

In regulatory news, Deadline has an exclusive on new anti-harassment laws now in force across the UK. Employers are now liable for harassment of staff, cast and crew by third parties, meaning production companies and studios are actively preparing their compliance procedures. Questions remain about implementation, but the impact on how productions operate is expected to be significant.

Realscreen reports that California Governor Newsom has proposed a billion-dollar tax credit for film and television production. This would be the largest capped credit in the US, and notably brings unscripted programming into the funding mix for the first time, including large-scale competition series.

The Beetz Brothers, producers of the crime documentary Clangold premiering on HBO Max next week, have welcomed the appointment of Casey Bloys as Skydance's content chief. They've described it as a strong sign that the HBO brand will continue producing high-end, mainstream content following the Paramount and Warner Bros merger.

Modern Times Review reports that Who Let the Docs Out is now accepting applications for its Autumn Grants scheme. Documentary filmmakers at any stage of development can apply for between four thousand and fifty thousand dollars, with expressions of interest due by November 13th.

And Realscreen tells us that Netflix has acquired Canadian rights to Banchi Hanuse's documentary Ceremony, scheduled to premiere on the platform in 2027.

That's your Indie Brief for today. Keep making great independent stories.
