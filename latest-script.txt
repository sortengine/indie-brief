Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Saturday 14 November.

The European Film Academy has announced nominations for the thirty-ninth European Film Awards, taking place in Greece in January twenty twenty-seven. Paweł Pawlikowski's Fatherland leads with eleven nominations, followed by La Bola Negra with ten nominations, and Cristian Mungiu's work also featuring prominently across the sixteen key categories. This is one of Europe's most prestigious awards, so these nominations are significant for independent producers and financiers tracking the continent's most acclaimed films.

In documentary news, Realscreen reports on a strategic partnership between French production company Ah! Production and Paris-based distributor Balanga. They're developing fifteen hours of AI-assisted history and science documentaries, with Radio-Canada already signed on as a pre-buyer. This signals growing interest in how emerging technology can support documentary production pipelines.

Netflix has set a December eleventh premiere date for the feature documentary Jean-Michel Basquiat, following its Tribeca premiere. The film profiles the iconic New York artist who achieved international acclaim in the nineteen-eighties. This demonstrates continued streaming investment in artist-focused documentary content.

The World Congress of Science and Factual Producers has announced a new location for its twenty twenty-seven event. This annual gathering of international producers, commissioners and distributors remains essential for the specialist factual and documentary community.

Finally, a note on practical matters: the FAA's nationwide ground stop on Friday affected thousands of flights, impacting attendees at the American Film Market. While the stoppage was lifted after nearly five hours, organisers of major industry events this weekend have had to scramble to accommodate travel disruptions for delegates.
