Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Sunday 4 October.

Modern Times Review reports that greenstories, the Asian environmental film lab and pitching forum, is returning to Goa for its third edition from 11 to 17 November. More than 150 submissions have been narrowed down to 22 documentary projects that will participate in the event, which combines a lab with pitching opportunities for emerging filmmakers working on environmental subjects across Asia.

Also from Modern Times Review, two documentaries are marking the 25th anniversary of 9/11 by returning to ground zero. The Responders gathers testimony about the attacks and calls for a reopened criminal case, while 32 Frames follows a single roll of film recovered from the rubble to the person who shot it, offering distinct approaches to revisiting this significant historical moment.

Deadline reviews Tom McCarthy's new film A Statement, which premiered at the New York Film Festival. The film centres on a 1980 Florida conference examining carbon dioxide emissions and climate change, bringing together politicians, academics, scientists and environmentalists. Despite its potentially dense subject matter, Deadline praises McCarthy's humanist approach, highlighting strong ensemble performances from Paul Rudd and Paul Giamatti.

And POV Magazine features an interview with director Beatrice Gibson about her film At Night. Gibson discusses creating an intersectional portrait of Parisian women while drawing on literary references, offering insight into her documentary practice and artistic influences.
