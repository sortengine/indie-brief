Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 30 September.

Channel 4 is teaming with the Stroke Association on a new documentary series called Trauma: My Broken Brain. According to Deadline, the series follows three young survivors through the aftermath of stroke and their journey back to independence. It's a timely commission given that someone in the UK suffers a stroke every five minutes.

In market news, Realscreen reports that Mongrel Media has acquired the film NAZA for a Canadian theatrical release starting in October. The documentary from directors Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor will roll out across Canadian cinemas this month.

At the production level, Cineflix has appointed Stephanie Fast as head of production. Fast, the former president of Frantic Films, joins the content producer and distributor in a senior role following the retirement of Sherri Rufh.

Also from Realscreen, BBC Studios is expanding in the Benelux region with the creation of a new in-house production base and a dedicated development team, marking the next phase of growth for the broadcaster in that territory.

HBO has commissioned a supernatural true crime documentary series called The Pet Cemetery Murders: A Ghost Story, set to premiere on October 11th.

And looking ahead to MIPCOM next month, the B2B rights platform Vuulr is relaunching with new ownership and investment, returning to the international market with new leadership.
