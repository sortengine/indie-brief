Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Thursday 26 November.

Deadline reports that Wild Horse Nine and Blueprint Pictures, the producers behind Three Billboards Outside Ebbing Missouri, have secured English-language remake rights to the German comedy No Hit Wonder. The story follows a fallen rock star who ends up in a psychiatric ward and helps the resident choir achieve national success. It's a timely acquisition as the dramedy format continues to find audiences across territories.

Realscreen reports that production is underway on a feature documentary about Napa Valley, bringing together London-based Docsville Studios, Paris-based PMG Productions, and UK production company Slam Films. The film will explore the people and rivalries behind the region's rise as a wine powerhouse.

Also from Realscreen, Toronto-headquartered Blue Ant Media has reported strong financial results, with fourth-quarter revenue rising 163 percent year-on-year to 160 point 1 million Canadian dollars. The streamer and production company also released full-year results showing growth across the business.

Screen Daily has reported that the UK's Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority has expanded its membership to include US streamers, Paramount Skydance, and production giant Banijay. The move follows increased industry support in the wake of recent high-profile misconduct cases.

And finally, Realscreen reports that the Banff World Media Festival has announced that executive director Jenn Kuzmyk is stepping down from her role. Kuzmyk was also serving as SVP and publisher of Realscreen.
