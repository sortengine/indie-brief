Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Sunday 15 November.

Modern Times Review covers Concrete Turned to Sand, a new documentary by Jessica Johnson and Ryan Ermacora exploring oyster farming on Canada's Cortes Island. The film offers a portrait of the fragile relationship between people, land and ocean, and will be of interest to documentary producers working in ecology and environmental storytelling.

Deadline reports on two indie films making waves at Contenders Los Angeles. Wicker, directed by Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson and based on Ursula Wills' short story, stars Alexander Skarsgård and Olivia Colman. Skarsgård praised the script's social commentary on feminism, saying it avoided being too preachy. Also at Contenders, Guy Nattiv's cult thriller The Liberation stars Carrie Coon and Lily James. Nattiv based the film on his own grandmother's story, and both actresses spoke about how understanding their characters' motivations was key to humanising what could have been one-dimensional roles.

Deadline also reports on The Violinist, an animated feature about a woman searching for her long-lost love after wartime separation. Composer Ricky Ho, working alongside Isabel Latorre, revealed the painstaking creative process behind the film's central sonata, spending two months crafting just the first four notes.

Finally, Deadline covers Danny Boyle receiving the Deadline Legend Award. The Oscar-winning director reflected on his diverse filmography spanning Trainspotting, 127 Hours, Slumdog Millionaire and beyond, discussing the common architectural threads running through his work across multiple genres.
