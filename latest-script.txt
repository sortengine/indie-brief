Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Tuesday 17 November.

Deadline reports that a French vertical video drama app called Shortstory has opened a one point one million dollar funding round to accelerate across the French-speaking world. The platform has already produced fifty four original series and accumulated more than ninety million views, showing strong potential in the growing microdrama space.

The Brit List has unveiled its 2026 selection, according to Screen Daily. The list includes thirteen scripts across feature film and television. Among the highlighted projects are a Cornish crime saga inspired by Taylor Sheridan and an adaptation of Joseph Conrad's The Secret Agent, alongside a healthcare chamber piece titled Do No Harm from director Polly Findlay.

Rola Bauer, former Amazon MGM Studios executive, is stepping into the role of Creative Director at the Monte Carlo TV Festival, Deadline reports. The festival has also expanded its advisory board with seven new members, including representatives from major talent agencies.

Netflix has renewed Polish medical drama Anaesthesia for a second season, following strong performance on the platform. The series hit the top ten in Poland immediately after launch and currently ranks fifth on Netflix's global chart, Deadline reports.

The BBC and Canada's CBC have confirmed their television adaptation of E.M. Forster's A Passage to India, Deadline reports. The series will be directed by Emmy winner Richie Mehta and stars Ali Fazal, Fiona Shaw and Tom Mison, examining the British Raj through the character of Aziz.

Screen International has launched Arab Rising Stars in partnership with the Film Committee at Media City Qatar and the Doha Film Festival, establishing a long-term commitment to championing Arab talent across the region.
