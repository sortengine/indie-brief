Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 30 September.

Deadline reports that Channel 4 is teaming with the Stroke Association on a new documentary series called Trauma: My Broken Brain. The series follows three young stroke survivors through their recovery journey, exploring the psychological and physical aftermath of stroke. Every five minutes someone in the UK suffers a stroke, making this an important commissioning move for the broadcaster.

Also at the commissioning desk, Realscreen reports that Investigation Discovery has greenlit a two-hour special called Toxic, hosted by Elizabeth Chambers and building on her existing ID series of the same name.

HBO is setting a supernatural-themed true crime documentary series called The Pet Cemetery Murders: A Ghost Story from Lightbox, premiering October 11th. It's a three-part series heading into the Halloween season.

On the distribution front, Screen Daily reports that the Venice Special Jury winner NAZA, an award-winning Gaza documentary by Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, has landed a UK and Ireland theatrical release and will stream on The Guardian. Realscreen adds that Mongrel Media has acquired the film for a Canadian theatrical release starting October 2nd.

At MIPCOM, Deadline reports that Israeli production company Dori Media is bringing its largest unscripted slate to date, including game shows The Hive and You Should Know, the docu-series My Hottest Years, and the primetime reality format New Love.

Finally, Screen Daily reports that Ava DuVernay's documentary 814 is among the first hundred films selected for IDFA, the International Documentary Festival Amsterdam, which takes place from November 12th to 22nd.

That's Indie Brief. Keep creating, keep commissioning.
