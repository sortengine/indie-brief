Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Tuesday 27 October.

Realscreen reports that Prime Video UK and Ireland has commissioned a true crime feature documentary from Sphere Abacus and Welsh production company Yeti Television. The ninety-minute film, Surviving the London Nail Bombings, is now in development.

Also from Realscreen, documentary filmmaker Alex Gibney is making his four-hour film Musk available on various video-on-demand platforms, citing what he calls social media platform censorship as his reason for the early VOD release.

Deadline reports that Macedonian filmmaker Milcho Manchevski is bringing his second feature of twenty twenty-six to the Tokyo Film Festival this week. Sister Brother Manhole Cover, which Manchevski also wrote, screens in competition and follows two siblings living on the outskirts of post-Communist Macedonia. In related festival news, Parallax Films has boarded international sales on Fuxi: Four Tales Of Feasting, directed by Chinese artist-filmmaker Qiu Jiongjiong, ahead of its world premiere in Tokyo's main competition section.

At the Tokyo Film Festival, Australian filmmaker Rolf de Heer, who is president of this year's competition jury, discussed the significance of A-list status for film festivals. Deadline reports that Tokyo has recently had its A-list rating reaffirmed by the International Federation of Film Producers Associations, known as FIAPF.

And finally, a major UK partnership: Deadline reports that Disney and Channel 4 have struck a landmark content deal in which the Channel 4 linear network will become available on Disney Plus in the United Kingdom, described as a UK first and one of the biggest deals of its kind in UK television history.
