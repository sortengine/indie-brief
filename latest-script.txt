Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Saturday 21 November.

Screen Daily reports that UK producer Rebecca O'Brien has called for radical changes to revitalise the struggling independent film sector. Speaking at the British Screen Forum, O'Brien proposed quotas for streamers and less interference from funders, highlighting the pressures facing indie producers in a rapidly consolidating market.

Realscreen reports significant personnel moves in the production sector. Andrea Scrosati, group chief operating officer and CEO of Fremantle's continental Europe operations, is stepping down at the end of January to launch a new entrepreneurial venture. Scrosati was a key architect of Fremantle's film investment strategy. Meanwhile, Kit Morey, commissioning editor of unscripted at UK broadcaster Channel 5, is departing on April 9th next year.

On the documentary front, Realscreen reports that the 39th International Documentary Festival Amsterdam has announced its awards. The Year of the Ghost and Memories of Lightning won the top prizes at IDFA, one of the world's most important documentary markets.

National Geographic has greenlit several new documentary projects. Realscreen reports the network has ordered films from Wonderhood, Curious Films, Passion Pictures and Muck Media, covering subjects including the Unabomber Ted Kaczynski, a hantavirus outbreak and the 2009 hijacking of the Maersk Alabama cargo ship.

Also on the commissioning front, Realscreen reports that HBO is set to premiere a new four-part documentary series called My Favorite Murder, following the hosts of the popular crime and comedy hybrid podcast.

Finally, Screen Daily reports that the Film Committee at Media City Qatar and Doha Film Festival have launched a new showcase called Screen International's 2026 Arab Rising Stars, spotlighting emerging talent from the region.
