Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Saturday 17 October.

Screen Daily reports that European film and TV organisations have issued a joint statement criticising a proposed cut to the AgoraEU cultural fund. The funding would be reduced from eight point six billion euros to six billion euros, which organisations say would be devastating for the screen sector across Europe.

On the funding front, Screen Daily has been speaking with director Carol Morley about how she raised private financing for her feature film Seven Miles Out, which marks her first feature made without public funding support. Meanwhile, veteran filmmaker Mike Leigh has expressed concern about the lack of creative freedom for young filmmakers in the UK, telling Screen Daily that while there is plenty of money available, financiers are often reluctant to relinquish creative control.

In industry news, Realscreen reports that Netflix has appointed Peter Rice, the former chairman of Walt Disney Television, to the role of chief content officer, following the departure of Bela Bajaria. The move has prompted analysis from industry observers about what the leadership change might mean for the streamer's content strategy.

Also from Realscreen, Outerlight Entertainment, previously known as First Look Entertainment, has acquired indie studio Bleecker Street Media, fresh from the release of Alex Gibney's documentary Musk.

And finally, the National Film Board of Canada has released the documentary There Are No Words, which filmmaker Min Sook Lee has made examining a personal family story. The film is now available to stream from the NFB.

That's your Indie Brief for this Saturday morning.
