Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Sunday 18 October.

Modern Times Review reports that the thirteenth Bozcaada International Festival of Ecological Documentary wrapped up this week after running from October seventh to eleventh. The festival presented four awards on Saturday, and in her closing speech, Festival Director Petra Holzer called for continued accountability from politicians and corporate interests. It's a timely reminder of documentary's role in holding power to account.

Also from the festival circuit, Screen Daily has been speaking with Sao Paulo festival director Renata de Almeida about the festival's track record of supporting new talent. De Almeida emphasised that the film itself is always the star, and that audience response is what truly energises a festival. A useful perspective for filmmakers thinking about where to premiere their work.

In other news, Deadline reports that actor Michael Forest has passed away at the age of ninety seven. Forest had an extensive career in character acting, appearing in numerous Roger Corman B movies and lending his voice to anime projects. He was also known for his role as Apollo in the original Star Trek series, as well as guest appearances across decades of television including The Twilight Zone and The Dick Van Dyke Show. A significant figure in film and television history.

Those are your main stories this morning. Keep an eye on festival announcements and remember that documentary continues to be a powerful tool for investigative storytelling and social impact.
