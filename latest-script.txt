Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Saturday 24 October.

Realscreen reports that Neon has joined The Guardian and mk2 Films to expand the reach of the documentary NAZA, directed by Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor. The move comes as the film continues to build momentum in the festival circuit.

Also from Realscreen, Canal Plus has acquired French rights for an extended version of The Death and Love of Claude Lanzmann, expanding on Adam Benzine's Oscar-nominated short documentary Claude Lanzmann: Spectres of the Shoah.

The Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival is ramping up for its 30th edition. Modern Times Review reports that the festival has unveiled the 20 films competing in Doc at PÖFF, alongside seven special screenings, offering a significant platform for documentary filmmakers.

Looking ahead to festivals, MajorDocs 2026 opens on 9 November in Palma with a programme built around the theme A World on Fire. The festival launches with Jean-Gabriel Périot's Une Jeunesse Allemande.

At Netflix, there's significant movement in the film division following the arrival of Peter Rice at the streamer. Screen Daily is asking what Rice's appointment could mean for Netflix's film culture, with particular interest in who will replace Dan Lin as film head.

Finally, on the festival circuit, Renate Reinsve has been discussing her work on Fjord, Cristian Mungiu's Cannes winner. The Norwegian actress, who won best actress at Cannes in 2021 for The Worst Person in the World, spoke about stepping into a completely different world with the Romanian director.
