Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Friday 27 November.

Realscreen reports that the microdrama business is projected to reach global revenues of twenty billion dollars by twenty twenty-six. While the sector continues to grow in both the US and international markets, producers should be aware that marketing costs and monetization challenges are impacting this nascent medium.

Also from Realscreen, Channel 4 has commissioned a new documentary about disgraced journalist Huw Edwards from Oxford Films, following his conviction for making indecent images of children. The former BBC newsreader will face questions in the film.

In funding news, Deadline reports that Canal+ has launched a skills academy backed by the President of South Africa, Cyril Ramaphosa. The initiative aims to benefit between eight hundred and one thousand people in its first year, as Canal+ continues to expand its investment across the African creative community following its acquisition of MultiChoice.

Northern Ireland Screen has appointed Laura Livingstone as its new Chief Executive, taking up the role in January. Livingstone brings two decades of experience to the position, taking over from Richard Williams who is stepping down in December after more than twenty-three years.

Screen Daily reports that Spain and Austria are leading the way on gender parity in European cinema thanks to targeted funding approaches. A survey by France's Lab Femmes de Cinéma has examined a decade of gender equality policies across European film production.

Finally, the Encounters Documentary Festival in South Africa is now accepting submissions for its twenty-ninth edition in June. The South African festival welcomes both African and international submissions, with early bird entries closing on 17 December.
