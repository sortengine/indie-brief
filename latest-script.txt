Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 28 October.

The International Documentary Association has announced the shortlists for the 42nd IDA Documentary Awards. Deadline reports that features including You Can See Everything, To Hold A Mountain, and Nuisance Bear made the cut, though some anticipated documentaries were notably absent. The IDA Awards are a significant indicator of Oscar prospects, so these shortlists are worth tracking closely.

Speaking of Nuisance Bear, Deadline's Doc Talk Podcast has featured the directors of this polar bear perspective feature, which won the Grand Jury Prize for U.S. Documentary at Sundance and has become one of the most honored nonfiction films of the year.

Also in documentary news, Deadline reports that Diamond Diplomacy, a feature doc from filmmaker Yuriko Gamo Romer exploring Japanese-U.S. history through baseball, is receiving its first international screening this week at the Tokyo Film Festival after a year on the U.S. festival circuit.

Turning to the UK and European market, Realscreen reports that Channel 4 and Disney Plus UK have struck a content deal, bringing Channel 4 content to Disney Plus UK on a day-and-date basis. This represents a significant distribution opportunity for independent producers.

Also from Realscreen, Fox Nation has renewed Watershed Motion Pictures' docuseries God. Family. Football. for a fifth season, with eight episodes currently in production.

Screen Daily reports that industry experts believe now is the right time for China-Brazil co-productions, with calls to expand film sales between those markets. This could open new financing and distribution opportunities for independent producers.

Finally, Screen Daily notes that the 36th annual Gotham Film Awards nominees include The Invite, Clarissa, All Of A Sudden, La Gradiva, and Tender Loving Care, each earning four nominations. These awards often highlight independent and international cinema.
