Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 4 November.

Realscreen reports that Utopai Studios, an AI-native film and TV production company based in California, has appointed Hans Ku as chief product officer. Ku previously led AI strategy at Walt Disney Company, signalling a growing intersection between artificial intelligence and content production that independent producers may want to monitor.

In festival news, Deadline reports that Ashland, a feature documentary debut from Wyatt Maw, is screening in competition at DOC NYC. Shot on Super 16mm film over eight years, the film offers a panoramic portrait of life in a diverse American community and screens ahead of the festival's November programming.

At the Tokyo International Film Festival, Sister Brother Manhole Cover from Macedonian filmmaker Milcho Manchevski won the Grand Prix and a nineteen thousand dollar cash prize. The award underscores continued international recognition for independent cinema outside major Western markets.

Screen Daily reports significant developments around US production incentives. Donald Trump's backing, combined with strong bipartisan Congressional support, is creating favourable conditions for introducing a federal production incentive scheme. This represents a potentially major shift for independent producers considering US-based projects.

Easy Tiger, the production company behind Colin From Accounts, has acquired television rights to Dervla McTiernan's psychological thriller novel Three Reasons For Revenge, following a competitive auction. Shaun Grant, known for The Narrow Road to the Deep North and Snowtown, will adapt the screenplay.

Finally, the UK's Creative Cities Convention will relocate to Glasgow in twenty twenty-seven, backed by Screen Scotland. The move comes as the industry awaits the TV Festival's relocation from its fifty-year home in Scotland.
