Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 11 November.

Lightdox has acquired world sales rights to The Big Boom, ahead of its world premiere at the International Documentary Festival Amsterdam. The feature documentary by Swiss filmmaker Gabriel Tejedor explores the contradictions of a Bulgarian town caught up in Europe's shifting geopolitical order, where arms production for Ukraine's front lines is reshaping the local economy.

In festival news, Sydney Film Festival has opened submissions for its 74th edition running 9 to 20 June 2027. The festival is offering its biggest prize pool yet, with A$205,000 in prizes available. The submission deadline is 26 February 2027. Meanwhile, Modern Times Review reports that MajorDocs, Mallorca's documentary festival, opened this week on 9 November with films running through 14 November under the theme A World in Flames.

Realscreen reports that LumoTV, the Hertfordshire-based sign language streaming platform, has launched a new documentary division. The move expands the commissioner's slate and includes the feature Everest, while the platform also reports that The Greatest Duo has landed a French adaptation.

On the financing front, BBC Studios has appointed Sreya Biswas as head of its Natural History Unit. Biswas moves up from director of programmes and will report to Kate Ward, managing director of unscripted.

Lastly, Deadline reports that the documentary NAZA by Israeli filmmakers Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor is on track to become one of the most-watched documentary films on theatrical release in 2026. The Gaza documentary has been seen by a worldwide audience of 1.5 million across 47 territories.

That's Indie Brief, keeping you across the independent film and documentary world. We're back tomorrow.
