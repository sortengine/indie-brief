Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Sunday 22 November.

Screen Daily reports that the US is preparing to welcome international co-productions once a new tax credit is in place. A Trump advisor has indicated that Qatar should be first on the list of countries to partner with the US, signalling potential opportunities for European producers to explore co-production deals with American counterparts under these new incentive frameworks.

In co-production news, Deadline reports that Watermelon Pictures has sealed a strategic partnership with Qatar's Film Committee at Media City Qatar. The Chicago-based production and distribution company plans to develop and co-produce a slate of films telling stories from the Global South, marking a significant expansion into larger-scale mainstream production for the independent outfit.

Turning to filmmaker news, Deadline reports that Luca Guadagnino has confirmed his American Psycho adaptation is not happening. The Oscar-nominated director has stepped away from the project two years after beginning work with writer Scott Z. Burns on the reimagining of Bret Easton Ellis's novel. However, Guadagnino's documentary Artificial about OpenAI co-founders is coming to cinemas through Neon, exploring the emergence of AI technology and its cultural implications.

Finally, there's movement on AI regulation affecting creatives. Deadline reports that CAA chief Bryan Lourd is urging Congress to pass the No Fakes Act to protect everyone from nonconsensual digital replicas of their voices and likenesses, a safeguard that could benefit independent filmmakers and producers navigating emerging technology risks.
