Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 7 October.

The major industry story this week: Realscreen reports that the Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery merger has officially closed. Skydance chairman David Ellison says the two film divisions will operate independently to preserve their legacies, while the combined TV operation will maintain content from core brands including MTV and Food Network. Ellison also confirmed that HBO Max and Paramount Plus will eventually merge into a single service after an initial bundling phase. On the debt front, Skydance co-CEOs have insisted their nearly eighty billion dollar debt load is manageable and won't shortchange production spending.

Moving to documentary and factual content: Realscreen reports that the team behind the acclaimed 2018 documentary The Biggest Little Farm is bringing a twelve-episode follow-up docuseries to YouTube. Also, Blink Films has unveiled Ghost Ship Mystery for PBS's Nova strand and Arte in France and Germany, following archaeologists investigating a historical maritime mystery.

On awards and festivals: The Critics Choice Association will honour filmmakers Ron Howard and Jehane Noujaim at the Critics Choice Documentary Awards on November eighth in New York. And Deadline reports that the Oscar-contending documentary Ash and Crayons, Evgeny Afineevsky's examination of Ukrainian children affected by Russia's war, will have its theatrical release on World Children's Day, November twentieth, through Briarcliff Entertainment.

A note of concern from Screen Daily: the Dinard Festival of British and Irish Film is facing an uncertain future. The local mayor's office is undertaking a consultancy to assess whether the festival can continue beyond its 2026 edition, citing economic challenges.

Finally, Screen Daily reports that the Busan Film Festival has gained A-list status, with competition winners now qualifying for the Oscars.
