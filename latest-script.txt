Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Monday 19 October.

Deadline reports that Yeon Sang-ho's psychological thriller Paradise Lost has sold to 118 territories worldwide. The Train to Busan director's film has secured deals including Twin for Japan, The Jokers Films for France, and Well Go USA for North America. The film is premiering in the Spotlight on Korea section at the Hawaii International Film Festival.

In documentary news, Deadline reports that Alex Gibney's Elon Musk documentary has continued to push back against platform censorship. TikTok restored the film's promotional account after the platform claimed its suspension was the result of an error.

Also from Deadline: A24's documentary You Can See Everything, co-directed by and starring Nathan Fielding and following Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes, has opened to 15 million dollars, marking the biggest documentary debut in more than two decades since Fahrenheit 9/11. The film achieved over 2,000 sellouts with lines around the block for the three-hour feature.

The Woodstock Film Festival has concluded its 27th edition with the naming of Jessica Edwards as its new executive director. She takes over from co-founder Meira Blaustein. Kelly O'Sullivan and Alex Thompson's Mouse won Best Narrative Feature at the festival's awards brunch.

Finally, the BFI London Film Festival has unveiled its 2026 award winners, with prizes going to films from France, Thailand and the UK.
