Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Monday 23 November.

Deadline reports that The Dark Side Of Surfing, a new documentary investigation into allegations of sexual abuse and harassment in the international and US surfing industries, is in development. The project is led by BAFTA-winning documentarian Sarah Macdonald, CEO of Make Waves Films, and includes leading women's rights lawyer Ann Olivarius on the team.

The Rotterdam Film Festival has hired Pepijn Kuyper as its new Managing Director, according to Deadline. Kuyper will join the festival in mid-January 2027 and work alongside outgoing director Clare Stewart through to the conclusion of the 2027 edition, which runs from 28 January onwards.

Modern Times Review reports that dok.incubator, the international workshop for documentary rough cuts, has opened its 2027 call for submissions after a year's pause. The programme returns with its original three-session format, a wider range of accepted storytelling forms, and an earlier submission deadline of 11 January 2027.

Also from Modern Times Review, CIRCLE has selected ten participants for the inaugural Expanded Orbit Cohort, a training and co-production programme led by Katerina Cizek. The first cohort includes six projects from eight countries, ranging from a simulated colonial village to documentation of the 2023 Thessaly floods.

Ties That Bind, the training and co-production initiative for European and Asian producers, is returning to Busan next year, Deadline reports. The programme will run through partnerships with the Asian Contents and Film Market, the Korean Film Council, and Japan's Visual Industry Promotion Organization, with the first of two workshops taking place in 2027.

Finally, Deadline has learned that Screen Daily has published its guide to 50 films to watch during this awards season.
