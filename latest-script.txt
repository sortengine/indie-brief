Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Wednesday 30 September.

Modern Times Review has an interview with Gediminas Andriukaitis about Inconvenient Films, the Baltic region's only human rights documentary festival. He discusses twenty years of building the programme around impact, and the philosophy of bringing diverse audiences together. Andriukaitis emphasises that if you only invite people who already agree with you, you're just talking to a mirror. Worth reading if you're thinking about festival curation or the role of documentary in social change.

Deadline reports that Final Payment and Congo Boy have won top awards at the Pingyao International Film Festival in China. Chinese director Niu Niu's Final Payment took Best Film in the Fei Mu Awards, while Rafiki Fariala's Congo Boy won Best Film in the Roberto Rossellini Awards. Final Payment also secured Best Actor for Niu Jianrong. If you're looking at international co-production opportunities or Asian festival circuits, Pingyao is clearly a significant platform worth monitoring.

And finally, Deadline coverage of a major streaming executive shift: Cindy Holland, who was Chair of Direct-to-Consumer at Paramount Skydance, is leaving the company. This comes ahead of Paramount's pending acquisition by Warner Bros Discovery, with questions still swirling about who will lead streaming in the merged entity. Worth keeping an eye on if you have existing relationships or projects in development with either organisation.
