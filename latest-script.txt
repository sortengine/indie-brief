Good morning, this is Indie Brief for Friday 23 October.

Realscreen reports that the Grierson-Percy Documentary Grant recipients have been announced. The award winners are Lilyana Torres and Carlos Morales for I Want to Kill My Grandfather, and Jacqui and David Morris for Shooting with Music. These grants provide crucial funding for documentary creators at critical stages of production.

Also in the documentary world, POV Magazine reports that Cinema Eye Honors has announced its Unforgettables honourees and the longlist for the audience award, with 17 contenders in the running. That's a significant field to watch as awards season builds momentum.

DOC NYC has unveiled something new for the shorts community. Realscreen reports the festival has revealed three finalists for its brand new Louie Awards, named after Louis Wolfson and designed to support short films during the awards season push. If you're producing or financing shorts, this could be a valuable avenue for recognition and momentum.

Turning to production news, an indie sci-fi thriller called Telephone has wrapped filming. Deadline reports that the film stars Laysla De Oliveira and Riccardo Scamarcio, was directed by Anthony Scott Burns, and shot in Sudbury, Ontario. Notably, Gale Anne Hurd is among the executive producers. The film follows a woman who is abducted by mysterious neighbours.

And finally, Greenwich Entertainment has acquired North American distribution rights to Spin Wars, according to Realscreen. The film is the feature directorial debut from five-time Emmy winner Philip Byron, so that's a significant milestone in the independent space.
